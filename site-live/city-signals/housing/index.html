Housing and neighborhood services in San Francisco

Are city services keeping pace where housing is concentrated?

Explore how homeless-designated affordable housing, neighborhood service requests and independently inspected street conditions vary across San Francisco's 41 neighborhoods.

Neighborhood-level comparisons only. They describe patterns across neighborhoods and do not show that housing, its residents or any facility cause a complaint.
How to interpret this analysis

What "housing" means here. Homeless-designated affordable housing units in developments financed or required by the city's housing office (MOHCD), counted when construction is complete. This is not a complete inventory of supportive housing, shelters, navigation centers, master-leased buildings or scattered-site units.

Different windows. Each source covers a different period. The window is shown next to every comparison.

Rates are events, not people. "Requests per 1,000 residents" counts reports; one person or one location can generate many. Commercial districts with few residents and many visitors show high per-resident rates, so check the per-area and per-business views too.

Inspections are a sample. The Controller inspects sampled routes; neighborhoods without inspected routes are shown as "not inspected", never as clean. Averages come with a 95% margin of error.

Privacy. No addresses or site locations are shown. Sites the city marks confidential are excluded.

Methods and limitations

  • Housing. Homeless-designated affordable housing units are the homeless unit counts in MOHCD's affordable housing portfolio, for developments marked construction complete; units under rehabilitation are counted separately. Dates are construction completion dates, which can differ from when residents moved in.
  • 311 response. Three separate measures: the share of closed requests that took 7 days or more to close; the share of requests still open more than 7 days after they were filed; and the median hours to close, from exact timestamps over the latest 90 days. Closure can be administrative rather than a confirmed fix.
  • Street conditions. The Controller's maintenance standards program rates sidewalk litter on sampled routes from 1 (none) to 5 (heavy); higher means more litter. A route covering several neighborhoods counts toward each of them; the citywide average counts each inspection once. Margins of error are 95% intervals.
  • Normalization. Population from the American Community Survey 5-year estimates; land area from the city's analysis neighborhood boundaries; commercial activity from active registered business locations. Neighborhoods with fewer than 2,000 residents are left out of per-resident rates.
  • Timing. The construction-completion strip under the 311 timeline shows when units were completed. Many things change in a neighborhood at the same time, so the chart shows timing only.
